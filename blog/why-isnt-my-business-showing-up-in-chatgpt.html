Why isn't my business showing up in ChatGPT? (and how to fix it)

7 min read·WeEvolveIT

Your business isn't showing up in ChatGPT, usually because your content isn't structured for AI to extract and cite. How to check it and get mentioned.

  2. 02Why your business isn't showing up in ChatGPT

Your business isn't showing up in ChatGPT usually for one reason: your content isn't structured for AI to extract and cite it. ChatGPT, Perplexity, and Gemini don't show a list of ten blue links; they write an answer and name a handful of sources. If yours isn't easy and trustworthy to cite, you're left out of the conversation, right where buying decisions now happen.

The good news: it's fixable, and not with a trick. It's about making your site the easiest, most trustworthy thing for an AI engine to cite. Here's why you're not showing up, how to check how bad it is, and what to do about it.

Key takeaways
  • You don't show up because your content isn't written in blocks AI can extract and cite.
  • ChatGPT names only a few sources per answer: if yours isn't clear and trustworthy, you're not one of them.
  • The five common causes: non-extractable content, thin authority, not being in the sources AI reads, vague data, and blocked AI crawlers.
  • Checking is free: ask ChatGPT and Perplexity about your industry and see if you come up.
  • The fix: answer directly and early in a self-contained block, back it with data and schema, and build authority.
  • Ranking #1 on Google is no longer enough; the new front page is being the source AI cites.

First: run the test yourself

Before any theory, prove it. Open ChatGPT or Perplexity and ask the way a customer would: "what are the best [your industry] companies in [your city]?" or "recommend a [your product or service]." Read the answer and notice who it names.

If your business isn't among the cited sources, that's not bad luck, it's a signal. The engine found content that was easier to extract and trust than yours. The question is no longer whether you rank, but whether the AI answer your customer is reading cites you.

Why your business isn't showing up in ChatGPT

When we audit sites that "don't show up in AI," it's almost always a mix of these five causes:

CauseWhat it means
Your content isn't extractableThe answer is buried under introductions. AI pulls short, self-contained blocks; if yours isn't one, it takes someone else's.
You lack authorityEngines cite sources they trust. Without authority signals (thorough content, references, a consistent entity), you're not a candidate.
You're not in the sources AI readsAI synthesizes from your site, reviews, directories, and mentions. If your presence there is thin, there's nothing to cite you from.
Your information isn't concreteWith no numbers, prices, data, or clear location, your page gives the engine nothing citable. Vague doesn't get cited.
You block AI crawlersIf your robots.txt or your site stops crawlers like GPTBot or PerplexityBot from reading you, you can't be cited, full stop.

Most pages fail on the first two. They're written to impress a human skimming, not so an engine can extract a clean answer. Size doesn't protect you either: in our AI Visibility Index 2026, 65 of Mexico's largest companies averaged 47/100 for AI citability.

How to check how bad it is

Don't guess, measure. Three quick checks:

  1. The direct test. Ask ChatGPT and Perplexity the 3 or 4 questions your ideal customer would ask. Note who shows up and who doesn't.
  2. The technical test. Confirm your site doesn't block AI crawlers and that your key pages carry structured data (schema).
  3. The content test. Open your main pages: is the first answer in the first 50 words, or do you have to scroll three paragraphs to find it?

How to get AI to actually mention you

Showing up in ChatGPT isn't a hack: it's making your content the easiest, most trustworthy thing to cite. The playbook:

TacticWhy it works
Answer in the first 40–50 wordsAI engines extract the clearest, earliest direct answer. Don't bury it.
Write self-contained blocksEach paragraph should make sense quoted on its own, no "as we said above."
Add FAQ schema and structured dataThey tell the engine exactly which text answers which question.
Use data and named sourcesReal numbers and concrete references give you source-worthiness the AI can trust.
Build topical authorityCover your subject thoroughly so AI treats you as the entity to cite, not a stray page.
Let AI crawlers inIf a crawler can't read you cleanly, it can't cite you.

The pattern mirrors classic SEO, with a twist: instead of writing to win a click, you write to win a citation. This is called GEO (generative engine optimization) and AEO (answer engine optimization). If you want the definitions, we break them down in What is GEO & AEO?. For the full step-by-step on becoming a cited source, see How to rank on ChatGPT. For Perplexity and Google specifically, see how to get cited by Perplexity and Google AI Overviews.

How long does it take?

It depends on where you start. If your site already ranks well on Google, structuring your content for AI can start paying off in weeks, because generative engines tend to pull from pages that already have authority. If you start from a weak site, you have to build that base first, and that's a matter of months. It's not a switch you flip: it's a layer that compounds over time.

GEO and AEO don't replace SEO

A common misread: this doesn't replace SEO, it stacks on top of it. AI engines usually cite pages that already rank well, so solid SEO stays the floor. GEO and AEO are the layers you build above it to stay visible as search shifts from a list of links to a single answer. That's why we offer generative engine optimization services, bake all three into our SEO service, and build this site that way from the foundation up. If an AI engine cited the page that brought you here, that's the method working in real time.

The bottom line

Your business isn't showing up in ChatGPT because your content isn't built for AI to extract and cite it: the answer is buried, authority is thin, or the engine has nothing to pull you from. The fix isn't a trick, it's making yourself easy and trustworthy to cite: answer directly and early in a self-contained block, back it with data and schema, let AI crawlers in, and build authority. Run the test today, ask ChatGPT about your industry, and if you're not there, you now know where to start.

Frequently asked questions

01Why isn't my business showing up in ChatGPT?

Usually one of five reasons: your content isn't written in blocks AI can extract, you lack authority signals the engine trusts, you're not present in the sources AI pulls from (reviews, directories, your own well-structured site), your information isn't concrete (no numbers, prices, or clear location), or your site blocks AI crawlers. ChatGPT doesn't show a list of links: it writes an answer and names a few sources. If yours isn't easy and trustworthy to cite, you're left out.

02How do I get ChatGPT to mention me?

Start each page with a direct, self-contained answer of 40 to 50 words, mark your content with FAQ schema and structured data, back your claims with numbers and named sources, keep your site crawlable and fast, and build topical authority by covering your subject thoroughly. AI engines cite pages that answer the question clearly and early, not ones that bury it under introductions.

03Is this the same as regular SEO?

No, but it builds on it. SEO wins you a spot in Google's list of links; showing up in ChatGPT wins you a citation inside the answer the AI writes. They share the same foundation (crawlable, authoritative, well-structured content), but AI search adds extractable answers, entity clarity, and source-worthiness that traditional SEO never had to prioritize. This layer is called GEO and AEO.

04How long does it take to show up in AI answers?

It depends on your starting point. If your site already ranks well in Google, structuring content for AI can start paying off in weeks, because generative engines tend to pull from pages that already have authority. If you're starting from a weak site, you have to build that base first, and that's a matter of months. It's not a switch: it's a layer that compounds over time.

05Does this matter for a local business or an online store?

Increasingly, yes, for both. People ask ChatGPT for product recommendations and ask AI assistants for nearby businesses, and in each case the engine cites only a few sources. For ecommerce, structuring your product data lets AI extract and cite specs and reviews; for local, it makes your service-area and review signals citable. It's a layer on top of your existing local or ecommerce SEO, not a separate strategy.

06Do I still need to show up in ChatGPT if I already rank #1 on Google?

Yes. Ranking #1 is no longer enough when more people ask the AI directly and read the answer without a single click. If that answer cites three competitors and not you, you're invisible right where the decision is being made, no matter how well you rank in traditional search.
